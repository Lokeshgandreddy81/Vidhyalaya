# Student Journey System

## Purpose

This document maps the ideal Cortex student journey across 500 hours. The journey is designed around transformation: from uncertain learner to capable developer.

## Journey Frame

Cortex should feel like a mentor who knows where the student is, remembers where they struggled, and gives them the next useful challenge at the right difficulty.

## First Hour

### Goals

- Build immediate trust.
- Let the student experience a real win.
- Establish that Cortex is interactive, not a content library.
- Diagnose baseline skill without making the student feel tested.

### Emotions

- Curiosity.
- Mild uncertainty.
- Hope that this will be different from other platforms.

### Obstacles

- Fear of looking incompetent.
- Tool overload.
- Confusion about where to start.

### Learning Needs

- A tiny mission with clear purpose.
- Fast feedback.
- Gentle correction.
- One reflection that makes progress visible.

### AI Support Requirements

- SARA introduces itself as a mentor, not a chatbot.
- SARA asks one goal question and one confidence question.
- SARA chooses a first mission with a high chance of completion.
- SARA explains mistakes without judgment.

## First Day

### Goals

- Complete 2 to 4 missions.
- Capture first skill memory records.
- Show the student how missions, notes, terminal, and SARA work together.
- Create a personal learning plan from observed behavior.

### Emotions

- Momentum.
- Relief after recovering from mistakes.
- Early confidence.

### Obstacles

- Trying to rush through content.
- Copying commands without understanding.
- Skipping reflection.

### Learning Needs

- Short missions.
- Repeated retrieval of core concepts.
- Early failure recovery in a safe environment.
- Reflection after every meaningful attempt.

### AI Support Requirements

- SARA detects repeated mistakes and adjusts hint depth.
- SARA asks the student to explain one concept in their own words.
- SARA writes a day-one journal summary.
- SARA recommends the next mission based on actual attempts.

## First Week

### Goals

- Establish learning rhythm.
- Build durable memory for foundational workflows.
- Introduce realistic scenarios.
- Begin skill graph calibration.

### Emotions

- More confidence.
- Productive frustration.
- Desire for harder challenges.

### Obstacles

- Forgetting commands between sessions.
- Avoiding weak topics.
- Mistaking familiarity for mastery.

### Learning Needs

- Spaced review.
- Mixed practice.
- Scenario-based transfer.
- Confidence calibration.

### AI Support Requirements

- SARA starts sessions with one targeted warm-up.
- SARA notices weak concepts and schedules review.
- SARA gives fewer direct answers when the student can reason through.
- SARA introduces "debug before answer" behavior.

## First Month

### Goals

- Move from guided execution to independent problem solving.
- Build project-level mental models.
- Introduce multi-step missions that span files, terminal, browser, and notes.
- Show meaningful growth in skill graph and journal.

### Emotions

- Ownership.
- Occasional frustration.
- Pride in recovering from harder failures.

### Obstacles

- Plateaus.
- Over-reliance on SARA.
- Difficulty connecting concepts across domains.

### Learning Needs

- Longer missions.
- Cross-skill scenarios.
- Failure simulations.
- Explicit reflection on judgment.

### AI Support Requirements

- SARA reduces hint availability gradually.
- SARA asks diagnostic questions before giving instructions.
- SARA compares current attempts to past mistakes.
- SARA recommends missions that address weak transfer skills.

## First 100 Hours

### Goals

- Develop practitioner habits.
- Build debugging confidence.
- Strengthen system thinking.
- Transition from learning isolated skills to shipping coherent work.

### Emotions

- Competence.
- Healthy impatience with toy examples.
- Identity shift toward "I can build."

### Obstacles

- Complex failure chains.
- Inconsistent self-assessment.
- Difficulty prioritizing what matters in a bug or feature.

### Learning Needs

- Realistic projects.
- Incident-style scenarios.
- Tradeoff reasoning.
- Peer-review style critique.

### AI Support Requirements

- SARA becomes more like a senior reviewer.
- SARA asks for hypotheses before interventions.
- SARA tracks independence: solved with no help, hint, guided correction, or direct answer.
- SARA generates deeper reflection and next-step missions.

## First 500 Hours

### Goals

- Become employable or project-capable.
- Demonstrate durable mastery across realistic problems.
- Build a portfolio of solved missions and reflections.
- Internalize debugging, planning, and learning habits.

### Emotions

- Confidence rooted in evidence.
- Calm under failure.
- Pride in accumulated skill.

### Obstacles

- Advanced ambiguity.
- Open-ended architecture decisions.
- Maintaining momentum without novelty.

### Learning Needs

- Capstone missions.
- Ambiguous requirements.
- Production-grade failures.
- Self-directed planning and review.

### AI Support Requirements

- SARA acts as mentor, reviewer, interviewer, and incident commander.
- SARA expects the student to propose solutions first.
- SARA challenges assumptions.
- SARA produces a mastery dossier: skills demonstrated, weaknesses remaining, and recommended next frontier.

## Journey Quality Bar

At every stage, Cortex must know:

- What the student can do independently.
- What the student can do with hints.
- What the student avoids.
- What the student forgets.
- What kind of challenge will make them stronger next.
