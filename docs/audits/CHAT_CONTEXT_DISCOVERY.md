# CHAT CONTEXT DISCOVERY

## Why does chat exist?
Chat exists not to provide a "ChatGPT alternative", but to serve as the intelligent friction-reducer in the learning process. It exists to unblock students when they are genuinely stuck, challenge them when they are coasting, and provide emotional validation when learning gets difficult. It exists to bridge the gap between static curriculum and individual understanding.

## When should students use it?
- When they encounter an error they cannot decipher.
- When they understand the "how" but not the "why" of a concept.
- When they are curious about how a concept scales in the real world.
- When they feel overwhelmed and need the problem broken down into smaller steps.
- When they need reassurance that they are on the right path.

## What problems should it solve?
- **Cognitive Overload:** Translating dense technical documentation into simple, relatable mental models.
- **Context Loss:** Remembering what the student was working on so they don't have to explain their setup.
- **Action Paralysis:** Moving a student from "I don't know what to do next" to "I am trying this specific hypothesis."
- **Isolation:** Providing the feeling of pair programming with a senior engineer.

## What should never require chat?
- **Basic UI Navigation:** Finding lessons or tracking progress.
- **Trivial Syntax Errors:** The IDE/Terminal should handle immediate syntax linting without AI intervention.
- **Module Summaries:** These should be provided inherently by the UI.
- **Copy-Pasting Code:** The chat should never be used as a blind code-generator; if the student is just copying and pasting, the chat has failed as a mentor.
